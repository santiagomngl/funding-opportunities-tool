### Which *problem or need* must the project address?

**Central object**

What is the principal thing the project must develop?

**The project must develop, demonstrate or validate scalable and replicable methods, tools or operational solutions that strengthen the resilience of power systems or integrated energy systems under stressed, degraded or emergency conditions.** It must address at least one intrinsic or external threat and one or more stages of anticipation, preparation, absorption, critical operation, recovery or adaptation.

**Project type**

Is the project a research programme, technology-development, operational demonstration, regional system pilot, policy initiative or public-private partnership?

**This is a two-stage transnational research, development and innovation grant for a project budgeted at EUR 2-5 million, including self-financing.** The main timing and submission conditions are:

- **Stage 1:** Submit the English pre-proposal through the CETPartnership Submission Platform by 8 October 2026 at 14:00 CEST, using the mandatory template.

- **Stage 2:** Invited consortia may submit a full proposal from 8 January 2027 until 11 March 2027 at 14:00 CET.

- **Decision:** The NWO funding decision is scheduled for 28 June 2027.

- **Project period:** The project should start between 1 September and 15 December 2027 and may last no more than 36 months; the call generally targets projects of 12-36 months.

**Problem definition**

What type of problem or need must justify the project? Where must it originate: with an operational user, industry, society, a region or a research community?

**The proposal must justify a resilience problem that could jeopardise reliability or quality of supply when the energy system operates beyond its normal design limits.** The problem may arise from:

- **Intrinsic vulnerabilities:** Structural weaknesses linked to high renewable penetration, variability, reduced inertia, limited voltage control or flexibility shortages.

- **External natural threats:** Extreme weather and its direct or longer-term climate effects, including storms, floods, snow and heatwaves.

- **External human threats:** Cyberattacks, sabotage or other deliberate disruption.

- **Cascading effects:** Interdependencies between energy carriers, digital systems and physical infrastructures that amplify a disturbance.

**Scope**

Which activities fall within the funding opportunity’s scope?

**Eligible work must focus on resilience in non-standard operating conditions and may develop methods and tools or demonstrate actionable measures.** Relevant activities include:

- **Threat assessment:** Probabilistic assessment, forecasting and monitoring of threats that exceed design ratings, including long-term climate scenarios.

- **Planning and restoration:** Tools for prevention, preparedness, rapid recovery and restoration of system performance.

- **Integrated assessment:** Methods that assess resilience across one or more stages of the resilience cycle.

- **Technology validation:** AI-based integration, distributed resources, storage, advanced protection and cybersecurity solutions.

- **Operational measures:** Resilient congestion management, continuity of mission-critical services, adaptive reconfiguration and coordinated multi-vector operation.

Routine maintenance, standard system optimisation or stand-alone technology development is relevant only when the proposal clearly extends it to measurable resilience under stressed, degraded or emergency conditions.

**Target user**

Whose need must the project address: system operators, regions, companies, consumers, public authorities or another group?

**The project must identify the Need Owners and intended users of its Key Exploitable Results, particularly system operators, infrastructure operators, end users and technology providers.** The proposed results should improve their ability to anticipate, withstand, manage and recover from severe disturbances.

**Public objectives alignment**

Which mission, policy objective, regional strategy or programme goal must the project support?

**The project must support the transition to climate neutrality by 2050 while strengthening energy-system resilience, security of supply and quality of supply.** It should create transnational added value and support the CETPartnership, the European Strategic Energy Technology Plan and the European Green Deal through stronger clean-energy innovation and system integration.

### How must *collaboration* be organised?

**Minimum and maximum number of partners**

What is the permitted or required consortium size?

**At least three funded Beneficiary Partners are required; the call states no maximum consortium size.** Self-financed Partners may participate in addition, but they do not replace the minimum of three Beneficiary Partners.

**Mandatory partners**

Must the consortium include universities, enterprises, SMEs, Need Owners, public authorities, end users or research institutes?

**A higher-TRL proposal or any proposal with demonstration activities must include at least one Need Owner as a formal consortium partner.** For a lower-final-TRL project focused mainly on modelling, planning tools or analytical methods, Need Owner participation in an advisory or steering capacity is recommended instead. No other organisation category is universally mandatory beyond the national eligibility rules for funded partners.

**Geographic distribution of partners**

Must partners come from a particular country or regions?

**The minimum three Beneficiary Partners must come from three different countries participating in CM2026-01, and at least two must come from EU Member States or Horizon Europe Associated Countries.** Each funded partner must also meet the rules of its own national or regional funding organisation.

**Coordinator or lead-applicant requirements**

What type of organisation may or must act as lead applicant?

**The transnational Coordinator must be a Beneficiary Partner and must remain unchanged from the pre-proposal deadline through the funding-selection decision.** Under the Dutch NWO route, one national main applicant is mandatory and national co-applicants are optional; the NWO main applicant must be an eligible researcher employed by an eligible research organisation.

**Distribution of effort, budget and research**

How must responsibilities, funding, work packages or research positions be distributed among partners?

**No single consortium partner may account for more than 60% of total project effort, and partners from one country may not collectively exceed 75%.** Effort is measured in person-months. The mandatory Reporting and Knowledge Community work package must also receive a specific allocation:

- **Minimum allocation:** At least 15 person-days per year, rising to at least 20 person-days per year when the total project budget exceeds EUR 2 million.

- **Expected ceiling:** No more than 3% of total project effort.

- **Budget treatment:** The work package must have corresponding resources in the project budget.

**Required intensity of participation**

Must TenneT participate through advice, co-design, co-development, demonstration hosting or formal consortium membership?

**TenneT is not named as a mandatory participant, but a formal Need Owner is mandatory for higher-TRL or demonstration proposals.** If TenneT fills that role, it should join as a formal self-financed consortium partner; for a lower-TRL modelling or analytical project, an advisory-board or steering-board role can be sufficient and is recommended.

### What is *TenneT’s position* within the consortium?

**Eligibility to apply or coordinate**

Can TenneT apply or coordinate, and under which conditions?

**TenneT cannot apply for NWO funding as the Dutch national main applicant or co-applicant and cannot coordinate the transnational proposal while participating only as a self-financed partner.** The transnational Coordinator must receive funding as a Beneficiary Partner, while the NWO national main applicant must be an eligible researcher at an eligible research organisation.

**Eligibility to receive funding**

Can TenneT receive funding, and which funding rate or cost rules apply?

**TenneT cannot receive NWO funding under this route.** The relevant Dutch funding conditions are:

- **Eligible organisations:** Eligible universities and public research organisations may receive 100% funding for basic research, applied or industrial research and experimental development.

- **Ineligible organisations:** NWO does not fund large, medium or small enterprises or public authorities through this route.

- **Maximum request:** Up to EUR 400,000 with one national main applicant, or EUR 600,000 when a national main applicant and at least one national co-applicant both request research-personnel costs.

- **Available Dutch budget:** NWO has EUR 2 million across the relevant supported modules and anticipates funding approximately five projects.

**Participation without funding**

Can TenneT participate as a self-financed or associated partner without receiving funding?

**TenneT may participate as a formal self-financed consortium partner and Need Owner without receiving NWO funding.** Its costs must be declared but will not be reimbursed through the call. At the full-proposal stage, TenneT must submit a Letter of Commitment confirming its active participation and role.

**Required TenneT contribution**

What contribution would make TenneT credible and useful within the project?

**TenneT would be most credible as the operational Need Owner that defines the resilience problem, validates the solution and owns a route to adoption.** A strong contribution could include:

- **Operational requirements:** Define relevant threats, stressed operating scenarios, performance thresholds and acceptance criteria.

- **System knowledge and inputs:** Provide asset and network expertise, selected data where feasible, and access to an appropriate test or validation environment.

- **Co-development:** Help align monitoring, forecasting, protection, control and data components with existing grid architecture and procedures.

- **Validation:** Assess whether the integrated solution improves preparedness, continuity, recovery or prevention of cascading failures.

- **Uptake:** Identify the interfaces, operating procedures, standards and internal owners needed to scale a successful result across assets or countries.

For a Reliability Centered Maintenance use case, the proposal should show a clear innovation step beyond routine condition-based maintenance by linking drone or sensor data to threat forecasting, stressed-system decision support, recovery and adaptation.

### What *type of innovation* must the project develop?

**Innovation threshold**

Must the project produce genuinely new knowledge or technology, or may it integrate and apply existing solutions?

**The work must be ambitious and go beyond the state of the art, but it may integrate and apply existing technologies when that integration creates a new and measurable resilience capability.** Routine optimisation or deployment without a clear resilience advance is outside the module's intended focus.

**Innovation object**

Must the project develop innovation in technology, system architecture, operational methods, market arrangements, governance models or a combination of these?

**Innovation may combine technologies and infrastructure, system integration and organisation, and system transformation or change.** CM2026-01 encourages a holistic approach covering technological solutions together with the interfaces, institutional frameworks, regulatory or market arrangements, user interaction, scaling and replication needed to make them operational.

**Starting and target maturity**

Which TRL or technical and operational maturity is expected at the beginning and end of the project?

**Demonstration projects must start at TRL 3 or higher and increase by at least one TRL level during the project.** The call does not set a single final TRL for every CM2026-01 proposal.

- **Demonstration route:** Validation and application may take place in a relevant environment or through advanced laboratory activities.

- **Modelling and analytical route:** TRL may not be fully applicable; the expected KERs must instead be usable tools such as models, software or APIs.

- **Tool quality:** These tools must use open-access platforms, follow recognised quality standards and ensure result traceability and system maintainability.

**Permitted development stages**

Must the project support research, engineering, prototyping, demonstration, deployment or only particular combinations of these stages?

**The module permits both research-oriented tool development and innovation-oriented validation or demonstration.** Work may cover forecasting, modelling, planning, engineering and integration, prototyping, advanced laboratory validation, demonstration in a relevant environment and preparation for operational uptake, provided every activity serves the defined resilience objective.

### Which *activities or work structure* must the project cover?

**Mandatory, optional and excluded activities**

Which activities are mandatory, optional or outside the funding opportunity required scope?

**Every project must address at least one resilience challenge and one or more stages of the resilience cycle, include the mandatory Reporting and Knowledge Community work package, and provide a credible exploitation strategy.** The activity boundaries are:

- **Mandatory:** A clear resilience objective, defined KERs, measurable outcomes, TRL-appropriate user engagement and the dedicated reporting and knowledge-community work package.

- **Optional:** Threat forecasting, integrated assessment, recovery tools, AI, protection, cybersecurity, distributed resources, congestion management, critical-service continuity or multi-vector coordination.

- **Outside scope:** Normal-operation optimisation or purely technology-focused development that lacks a stressed or emergency resilience dimension.

**Integration of activities**

Must the activities form one coherent system rather than separate or independent work packages?

**The work should form one coherent system-level resilience pathway linking the threat, intervention, validation evidence and route to uptake.** Individual technologies or work packages must show how they interact across the relevant physical, digital and organisational systems and how the proposal complements, rather than duplicates, standard optimisation or technology-specific work.

**User participation**

Must users participate in defining requirements, co-designing solutions, testing or validation?

**Need Owner participation must match the project's maturity and becomes formal consortium membership for higher-TRL or demonstration work.** The proposal should use Need Owners to define requirements, validate results and shape adoption; for lower-TRL modelling or analytical work, advisory participation is recommended.

**Demonstration setting**

Must the solution be demonstrated through a laboratory, simulation, living lab, real region or operational infrastructure?

**The call does not prescribe one demonstration format; it permits advanced laboratory activities or validation and application in a relevant environment.** The chosen setting must credibly represent the stressed, degraded or emergency condition being addressed and allow the proposal to measure resilience performance.

**Evidence**

What evidence must the demonstration produce to establish performance, feasibility or impact?

**The project must demonstrate measurable improvement in system resilience against a defined baseline, threat and operating scenario.** Depending on the use case, evidence may include:

- **Threat insight:** Improved assessment or forecasting of probability, severity, return period or consequences.

- **Operational performance:** Improved robustness, absorption capacity, continuity or quality of supply under disturbance.

- **Recovery:** Reduced recovery time or faster restoration of system performance and mission-critical services.

- **Integration:** Improved multi-vector coordination or prevention of cascading failures.

- **Transferability:** Evidence that the result can scale and replicate across relevant European energy-system contexts.

**Implementation and uptake**

Must the project address only development, or also implementation, adoption and wider uptake?

**The project must address development and a credible route to use beyond the funded work.** Its exploitation strategy must define the KERs, identify intended Need Owners and users, name the organisations responsible for advancing each result and describe the route to commercial transfer, open-access use or policy and institutional integration.

### In which *environment* must the project take place?

**Physical, digital or regulatory systems**

Within which physical, digital, market or regulatory system must the project operate?

**The project may address a power system on its own or an integrated multi-carrier energy system connecting electricity, gas and heating or cooling through physical and digital infrastructure.** It may also address the institutional, regulatory and market arrangements needed to operate resilience technologies effectively.

**Operational conditions**

Must development or testing take place under real operational conditions?

**Development and testing must represent realistic stressed, degraded or emergency conditions rather than routine system operation.** A live operational trial is not universally required, but a simulation, laboratory or relevant-environment test must be credible for the threat, system dependencies and performance claims being assessed.

**Safety, cybersecurity and data constraints**

Must project satisfy safety, cybersecurity, data-access or operational-continuity requirements specific to the funding opportunity?

**Every proposal must complete ethics and Do No Significant Harm self-assessments; cybersecurity and data protection should be addressed where relevant to the proposed solution.** Digital outputs should also consider open science and FAIR data principles. A TenneT-led use case should define proportionate access controls, data governance and operational-continuity safeguards for any asset or system data used.

**Integration with existing systems**

How must the project interact with existing infrastructure, technologies and organisational processes?

**Where the solution interacts with existing infrastructure, the proposal should explain how its components connect to energy assets, data systems, protection and control functions and organisational procedures.** For TenneT, this means defining interfaces, constraints and validation criteria so that monitoring, forecasting, automation and recovery measures work together without creating new operational or cascading risks.

### How must the project *scale, replicate or achieve uptake*?

**Pilot or repeatable solution**

Is the intended result an isolated pilot or a solution designed for wider use?

**The intended result must be scalable and replicable rather than an isolated pilot.** A site-specific demonstration may be used, but the proposal must show how the methods, interfaces and operating approach can transfer to other assets, systems or European contexts.

**Replication target user**

Where and by whom must the solution be replicated or transferable?

**Results should be transferable across European energy systems and to clearly identified Need Owners and users.** These may include system and infrastructure operators, end users, technology providers, regulators or other organisations responsible for operational or institutional uptake.

**Timing of replication**

Must replication occur during the project, or must the proposal only demonstrate that future replication is credible?

**The call requires a credible pathway to future scaling and replication but does not require full replication to be completed during the funded project.** The proposal should validate the transfer conditions during the project and identify who will advance the results afterwards.

**Standardisation and interoperability**

Must project satisfy or develop specific standards, interfaces or interoperability requirements?

**No named technical standard is imposed, but modelling and analytical tools must follow recognised quality standards and preserve traceability and maintainability.** Where multiple technologies, energy carriers or infrastructures interact, the proposal should specify the interfaces and interoperability conditions needed for safe and replicable operation.

**Application or market-introduction horizon**

Must result become operationally applicable or commercially available?

**No fixed commercialisation or operational-deployment deadline is set, but each KER must have a credible route to use beyond the project.** The route may be commercial transfer, open-access use or policy and institutional integration and should be proportionate to the target TRL.

**Implementation model**

Must a business, governance, ownership or implementation model support continued use?

**The exploitation strategy must explain how continued use will be organised and who will be responsible.** Depending on the result, this may require a business model, licensing or intellectual-property approach, open-access maintenance model, operational ownership arrangement or policy-integration pathway.

**Knowledge utilisation and dissemination**

Are knowledge-utilisation, dissemination, training or capacity-building activities required?

**Reporting, Knowledge Community participation, communication and dissemination are mandatory project activities.** The proposal and work plan must provide for:

- **Reporting:** A publishable factsheet, annual reports, a final report and participation in transnational surveys.

- **Knowledge Community:** Participation in the CETPartnership's co-creation activities through the dedicated work package.

- **Communication:** Objectives, key messages, target audiences, channels, responsibilities and resources proportionate to project size.

- **Project visibility:** Registration on DISCCO, a public project website or webpage and the required CETPartnership, EU and funder acknowledgements.

- **Open science:** Due consideration of open science and FAIR principles for research and innovation outputs.

**Post-project adoption**

Must someone adopt, operate, finance or maintain the result after funding ends?

**The proposal must name the organisation or organisations responsible for advancing each KER after the project and describe the intended uptake pathway.** For a TenneT use case, this should identify the internal owner, operational process, resources and partner handover needed to validate, maintain, deploy or scale the result.

### How must project deliver *change*?

**Concrete outputs**

Which concrete outputs must the project produce? Models, prototypes, tools, standards, datasets, methodologies or demonstrations?

**The proposal must define concrete KERs and deliver validated, scalable and replicable resilience results.** Depending on the selected route, outputs may include:

- **Assessment outputs:** Threat-assessment and forecasting methods, resilience methodologies, scenarios and supporting evidence.

- **Digital tools:** Models, software, APIs, data-processing or decision-support tools, particularly for lower-TRL analytical projects.

- **Validated solutions:** Monitoring, protection, AI, cybersecurity, storage or distributed-resource solutions tested for resilience performance.

- **Operational outputs:** Recovery and restoration tools, resilient congestion-management approaches, adaptive reconfiguration methods or multi-vector operating procedures.

- **Demonstration evidence:** Documented performance, transfer conditions and an exploitation package identifying responsible users and next steps.

**Operational effects**

What must improve for TenneT or another Need Owner? Monitoring, restoration, flexibility, interoperability or maintenance?

**The project must produce at least one measurable improvement in operational resilience under a severe disturbance.** Relevant effects include better threat monitoring and anticipation, reduced vulnerability, continuity during degraded operation, faster recovery and restoration, improved multi-system coordination and stronger operator preparedness. In an RCM use case, this could connect condition data from drones or sensors to resilience forecasting, intervention priorities and recovery decisions.

**System outcomes**

Which wider system outcomes must the project support? Transport capacity, grid availability, congestion reduction, renewable integration, affordability or environmental performance?

**The principal system outcomes are stronger grid availability, continuity and quality of supply, faster recovery and prevention of cascading failures.** The proposal should show how the result improves the system's capacity to withstand, manage, recover from and adapt to threats, rather than presenting routine capacity expansion or cost reduction as the primary outcome.

**Wider impact**

How must the project contribute to climate targets, industrial capability, regional development, knowledge networks or broader societal objectives?

**The project must contribute to a secure and resilient clean-energy transition compatible with climate neutrality by 2050.** Wider value may include:

- **Scientific:** New knowledge, methods, evidence and interdisciplinary capability for resilience planning and operation.

- **Technological and economic:** Stronger innovation capacity, system integration, operational uptake and competitiveness in clean-energy solutions.

- **Societal and environmental:** More reliable essential energy services, stronger stakeholder preparedness and climate-resilient high-renewable energy systems.

- **Transnational:** Shared knowledge, data, tools, resources and infrastructures that generate value beyond separate national projects.
